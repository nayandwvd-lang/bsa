# Model application to summon a document

Used when a document needed for the case is in the possession of the opposing party or a third party who will not produce it voluntarily. This page gives the model application and the notice-to-produce letter that should ordinarily precede it; the substantive rules behind both are worked out on the chapter pages linked below, not repeated here.

## Two documents, two purposes

1. **Notice to produce** — served on the party in possession *before* trial, under [s. 64](/chapters/chapter-05c#section-64-—-the-notice-to-produce-gateway). This is what makes secondary evidence admissible later if the original is withheld, and is a precondition under [s. 60, clause (a)](/chapters/chapter-05a#section-60-—-when-secondary-evidence-is-receivable). Serve this well before the hearing at which the document will be needed — a notice served the morning of trial rarely satisfies the "reasonable" standard s. 64 falls back on when no form is otherwise prescribed.
2. **Application to summon** — filed with the Court, used either against a third party who owes no independent duty to produce, or against a party who has ignored a notice to produce and from whom compulsion is now sought under [s. 165(1)](/chapters/chapter-10c#sections-159-to-168-—-corroboration-refreshing-memory-document-production-and-the-judge-s-own-power-to-question).

::: warning Drafting trap
A notice to produce and an application to summon are not interchangeable. Skipping the notice and going straight to an application against your *own opponent* can work, but skipping it also forfeits the s. 60(a)/s. 64 secondary-evidence gateway if the document is never produced — you may win the summons and still have no way to prove the contents if the original is later reported lost or destroyed before compliance. Serve the notice even where an application is also contemplated.
:::

## Model notice to produce

> To: [Opposing party / advocate on record]
> Re: [Case title and number]
>
> Take notice that you are called upon to produce, at the next hearing of the above matter on [date], the following document(s): [describe with precision — nature of document, approximate date, parties to it, and any other identifying detail].
>
> This notice is given under section 64 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023. In the event the said document(s) are not produced, [applicant] shall be entitled to give secondary evidence of their contents without further notice.
>
> [Signature, date, mode of service]

## Model application to summon a document

> IN THE COURT OF [designation]
> [Case title and number]
>
> APPLICATION FOR SUMMONING A DOCUMENT
>
> 1. The applicant states that [describe document] is relevant to the matter in issue at [identify the issue/fact in the pleadings the document bears on].
> 2. The said document is in the possession/power of [respondent to the application — opposing party or named third party], who has [failed to produce it despite notice dated ___ / no independent obligation to produce it save on the Court's summons].
> 3. The applicant is unable to produce the said document from any other source, and its production is necessary for the just decision of the case.
> 4. It is prayed that this Hon'ble Court be pleased to summon [named person] to produce the said document(s), described as [describe with precision], on [date] or such other date as this Court may direct.
>
> [Verification, signature]

## Checklist

1. Describe the document with enough precision that a summons can be complied with — vague descriptions invite an objection that the summons itself is bad.
2. State *why* the document is relevant, tied to a specific pleaded issue — a fishing application is liable to be refused.
3. If seeking to rely later on secondary evidence should production fail, confirm a proper s. 64 notice was already served — an application to summon does not itself satisfy s. 64.
4. Remember the consequence of the addressee's own refusal: under [s. 167](/chapters/chapter-10c#sections-159-to-168-—-corroboration-refreshing-memory-document-production-and-the-judge-s-own-power-to-question), a party who refuses to produce a document after notice cannot later use that same document in its own favour without consent or a court order — this cuts both ways and is worth pointing out to a reluctant opponent before filing.
5. If a document is produced and inspected under a notice to produce, and the requesting party calls for it to be exhibited, [s. 166](/chapters/chapter-10c#sections-159-to-168-—-corroboration-refreshing-memory-document-production-and-the-judge-s-own-power-to-question) binds that party to put it in evidence if the producing party insists — do not call for inspection casually if you are not prepared to have the document go in.
