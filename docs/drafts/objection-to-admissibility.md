# Model objection to admissibility — mode of proof vs. relevance

Two different objections get filed under one label, "objection to admissibility," and conflating them is the single most common way an advocate either objects too late or fails to object at all. This page keeps them separate and gives a model form for each. The legal test behind each track lives on its own chapter page — this page links to it rather than restating it.

## The two tracks

| | Objection to **relevance** | Objection to **mode of proof** |
|---|---|---|
| What it says | This fact is not one the Court may consider at all, whatever proof is offered | This fact might be admissible, but the *way* it is being proved is defective |
| Typical ground | The fact falls outside Chapter II's relevancy chain, or is barred by the parol evidence rule | Secondary evidence tendered without a foundation under s. 60; an electronic record tendered without a s. 63(4) certificate; a document tendered without proof of execution |
| When it must be raised | At any stage — a Court should not act on an irrelevant fact even if nobody objects, though a timely objection makes the point cleanly | **At the time the evidence is tendered.** A mode-of-proof objection not raised at that point is routinely treated as waived, because the defect could often have been cured on the spot (a witness recalled, a certificate produced there and then) |
| Canonical treatment | [Ch. VI — exclusion of oral evidence by documentary evidence](/chapters/chapter-06) for the parol evidence rule; the relevant [Ch. II](/chapters/chapter-02a) sub-page for the specific relevancy ground in issue | [Ch. V part I](/chapters/chapter-05a) (secondary evidence foundation) and [Ch. V part II](/chapters/chapter-05b) (the s. 63 certificate) |

::: warning Drafting trap
Do not object "this is inadmissible" without saying which track you mean. A judge who is handed an undifferentiated objection at the moment a chat-log printout is tendered may treat it as an objection to relevance (which the Court can revisit later) rather than the time-critical mode-of-proof objection it actually is — and by the time the distinction is noticed, the moment to insist on the certificate has passed.
:::

## Model objection — relevance track

> May it please the Court, the [document/fact/statement] now sought to be tendered by [party] is objected to on the ground of relevance. It does not fall within [the specific relevancy provision relied on by the tendering party — name the section], and its admission would [invite the Court to consider a fact / statement] that the Adhiniyam does not make relevant to the matter in issue. The objection is taken now, without prejudice to the right to renew it at the stage of final arguments should the Court be inclined to admit the [document/fact/statement] subject to relevance being argued later.

## Model objection — mode of proof track

> May it please the Court, before the [document / electronic record] now sought to be tendered by [party] is taken on record, objection is taken to the *manner* in which it is being proved, not to its relevance. [Select the applicable ground:]
> — No foundation has been laid under section 60 of the Adhiniyam for admitting secondary evidence of this document; the original has neither been produced nor accounted for.
> — This is a computer output within the meaning of section 63 of the Adhiniyam, and no certificate under section 63(4), in the form prescribed by the Schedule, has been filed along with it at this instance of tender.
> — Due execution of this document has not been proved in accordance with sections 65 to 73 of the Adhiniyam.
> This objection is taken at the earliest possible stage — the moment of tender — precisely so that the defect, if curable, may be cured now rather than argued about after the evidence has closed.

## Timing checklist

1. Identify **which** track applies before the evidence is tendered, not after — read the file in advance if the tender is anticipated.
2. For a mode-of-proof objection, object **on the record, at the moment of tender**. Do not wait for cross-examination or final arguments.
3. State the specific provision relied on (s. 60 clause, s. 63(4), or the relevant execution-proof section) — a generic "no foundation" objection is harder for the Court to rule on and easier for the other side to cure informally.
4. If the objection is overruled, note the overruling on the record; it may matter on appeal.
5. If the objection is to relevance, it may be renewed later — say so, so the Court understands it is not being waived by a provisional tender.
