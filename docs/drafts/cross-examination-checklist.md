# Cross-examination checklist — challenging authenticity

Used when the document or electronic record itself is genuine evidence *if* proved correctly, but its provenance is exactly what is in dispute — a signature that may not be the witness's own, a chat export that may have been edited, a copy tendered as if it were the original. The tests behind each question below are worked out in full on the linked chapter pages; this page is the question list to take into the box.

::: proviso Authenticity and admissibility are different fights
Winning an admissibility objection (no certificate, no notice) keeps a document out before the Court reads it. Cross-examination on authenticity is what you do once a document **is** in evidence, to persuade the Court it should carry little or no weight. Do not treat a successful cross-examination as a substitute for an admissibility objection you should have taken earlier — by the time cross-examination starts, the document is already on record.
:::

## For a physical document — execution and signature

Full test at [Ch. V part III](/chapters/chapter-05c#sections-65-to-73-—-proving-execution-signatures-attestation-and-comparison).

1. Does the witness admit the signature is theirs? If not, has execution been proved by any of the alternative routes — an attesting witness, comparison of handwriting, or a person acquainted with the signature?
2. If the document required attestation, was the attesting witness called, or is a permitted exception (death, unavailability, the registration proviso) actually made out on the facts — not merely asserted?
3. If comparison of handwriting or signature is relied on, was it done by a person with a genuine basis for familiarity, or by bare visual assertion?
4. Does the witness's account of *when* and *where* the document was signed hold up against other evidence already on record (other witnesses, contemporaneous documents, travel or attendance records)?
5. Is there any alteration, interlineation, or erasure on the face of the document that the witness cannot explain?

## For an electronic record — the certificate and the underlying device

Full test at [Ch. V part II](/chapters/chapter-05b#sections-61-to-63-—-admissibility-of-electronic-records).

1. Was a s. 63(4) certificate filed, in the Schedule form, at this instance of tender? If not, authenticity questions are premature — the record is not yet in evidence at all. See the [objection to admissibility, mode-of-proof track](/drafts/objection-to-admissibility).
2. Who is the certifying witness, and do they actually have personal knowledge of the device's operation — or are they signing a form prepared by someone else?
3. Was the device working properly throughout the relevant period, and was information fed into it in the ordinary course of business? Press on any gap, malfunction, or irregular use the witness concedes.
4. Does the stated hash value match a hash independently computed from the record as produced in Court? A mismatch, or an unwillingness to permit independent verification, is worth pursuing directly.
5. Who had access to the device or account between the event recorded and the extraction of the output — and can the witness rule out alteration during that window?
6. If the record is a copy of a copy (a screenshot of a screenshot, a forwarded message), can the witness actually speak to the originating device, or only to the copy in front of them?

## General questions that apply to both

1. Chain of custody: who has held the document or device since it came into existence, and is there a gap the witness cannot account for?
2. Motive: does the witness, or the party calling them, have a reason to have produced or altered the document favourably?
3. Consistency: does the witness's account of the document's provenance match what was pleaded, or what was said at an earlier stage of the proceeding (a prior statement, an affidavit, an earlier deposition)?

::: warning Drafting trap
Do not spend cross-examination time re-arguing an admissibility point that has already been ruled on. If the Court has admitted the record over an objection, that ruling stands unless successfully challenged on appeal — cross-examination at this stage should attack weight and reliability, not relitigate the ruling.
:::
